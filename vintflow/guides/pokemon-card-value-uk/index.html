How much is my Pokémon card worth in the UK?

The same Pokémon can appear on dozens of cards. A reliable estimate starts with the exact print, language, condition and finish, rather than just the character name.

Calculate your minimum selling price

1. Identify the exact card

Photograph the front straight on, with the name and bottom collector number visible. Take a separate photo of the back and any grading label. Write down the printed name, full collector number (including the denominator if present), set marking, language, and any special foil or stamp. A catalogue match can help confirm the artwork, but it does not prove authenticity.

For Simplified or Traditional Chinese cards, keep the original printed name and set markings. Do not assume a Japanese or English printing is the same edition just because its artwork looks similar. The collector number and set are especially useful when names are difficult to read.

2. Find comparable UK listings

On eBay UK, search for the character name, exact collector number, set and language. Try the printed name as well as the English name. Use the Sold items and Completed items filters where available. Compare the same finish and language; exclude bundles, replicas, unrelated editions and graded cards when your card is raw.

Record the item price separately from postage. An active asking price is not evidence of a sale. If a listing accepted an undisclosed offer, you may not know the final price. Recent comparable sales are more useful than an isolated high asking price.

3. Work out a reasonable range

Collect several relevant sold prices when possible. Check condition, dates and whether they represent the same print. Use the middle of the comparable prices as a starting point, with a lower and upper range reflecting the evidence. If you cannot find enough matches, report that the value is uncertain instead of inventing one.

Example for illustration only: three genuinely comparable sales at £8, £10 and £12 suggest a £10 midpoint before any condition adjustment. If purchase cost was £4 and seller-paid costs are £1, a £10 sale would leave £5 before tax or other expenses. These are fictional figures, not the price of a real card.

4. What if AI finds the card but no price?

Recognition and valuation are separate checks. VintFlow's private scanner may identify a card without finding suitable price comparisons. Confirm the exact print and use the manual research steps above. Never substitute a different language, foil or grade simply to produce a price. For graded cards, compare the same grading company, grade and print rather than raw-card values.
