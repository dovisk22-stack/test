How to identify vintage clothing

A practical checklist for dating clothing with labels, construction and historical references without assuming age from a logo.

Start with observable facts

Write down the maker, garment type, exact label text, labelled size and distinctive details. Photograph the garment in daylight and photograph each label separately. Note if the label was removed or replaced; missing evidence is not proof of an early date.

Look beyond the label

Closures, seams, fabric information and cut can help narrow an era, but manufacturers use old components and reissues reproduce older construction. Compare several clues with documented examples. The Vintage Fashion Guild's dating tips are a useful starting point.

Compare the references

Search both the label design and the brand's known history. Look for dated catalogues, fashion archives, documented labels and collector references. Avoid relying on one marketplace title written by another seller.

Write your conclusion carefully

If you can defend a particular period, describe the supporting evidence. Otherwise use wording such as 'vintage style' or 'era unconfirmed' rather than a precise decade. Keep screenshots or references for your own notes. Use the scanner for searchable details, not a certificate of age.
