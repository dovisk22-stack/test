Quick-sale versus maximum-profit pricing

Choose between faster stock turnover and a higher asking price while protecting your minimum profitable return.

Try free pricing toolOwner access
Public guides and pricing tools are open. The app is temporarily owner-only.

Define the goal before setting a price

A seller clearing out a wardrobe may value convenience more than a seller with money invested in stock. A lower asking price can make a listing more competitive, while a higher asking price may leave room for offers but may take longer. Neither guarantees a sale.

Calculate three scenarios

Suppose comparable asking prices cluster around £15 and your item and seller-paid expenses cost £6. An illustrative £12 quick-sale price would leave £6 before other costs; £15 would leave £9; £18 would leave £12 if a buyer pays that amount. This is arithmetic, not market research.

Set a negotiation floor

If the seller requires £5 profit on £6 of costs, the target floor is £11. A lower offer might not meet the goal. Check whether the market supports the floor, and be willing to revise expectations if necessary.

Measure actual results

Record final selling price and expenses, not just listing prices. Revisit categories with lots of unsold stock and avoid assuming all higher asks become higher profits. Try the quick-sale calculator and use the market price checker for context.
