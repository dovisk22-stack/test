Which vintage clothing brands retain value?

Learn to evaluate brand demand, particular models and condition rather than assuming any vintage label guarantees a good resale price.

Think in models, not logos

Brands such as Levi's, Carhartt, Ralph Lauren and Nike are useful examples to research, but price varies substantially between individual garments. A common recent T-shirt is different from a documented older collectible design. A recognisable name does not guarantee a premium.

Research before buying stock

Record the style, model, size and condition of a potential purchase. Check comparable completed sales where available and the number of competing active listings. Estimate the expenses, possible discounts and time to sell before deciding whether to buy.

Check context and provenance

For designer, collectible or rare garments, provenance and authenticity matter. Do not rely on a logo alone. Consult credible label archives or specialists, and avoid unsupported authenticity claims. The Vintage Fashion Guild resources help with historical research.

Track your own real results

Keep a record of brand, purchase price, fees, sale price and sale date. Over time your own realised margin and time-to-sale are more relevant to your specific stock than a generic list of supposedly valuable labels. Use the reseller workspace to track data you actually observe.
